Frequently Asked Questions

General (Mancora & Hotel)

What is the water and air temperature in Máncora?

Air and water temperatures vary throughout the year. Here's a general guide:

Air & Water Temperature Chart (Approx.)
Air: ~27°C-36°C (80°F-97°F) | Water: ~19°C-25°C (66°F-77°F)

It's generally warm year-round!

What clothing should I bring?

Casual beachwear is the standard: shorts, t-shirts, flip-flops, swimwear.

Bring a good hoodie or light jacket for cooler evenings, especially during the Peruvian winter months (approx. July-October).

During the summer months (approx. December – April), evenings are warmer, and you likely won't need the hoodie.

Is Mancora suitable for my non-kitesurfing partner or kids?

Yes, definitely! Mancora and La Maison Eco House offer plenty for everyone.

For Partners/Non-Kiters: Relax on the beautiful beaches, read by the pool, enjoy spa treatments, practice yoga, go surfing or stand-up paddling, take horse rides, or explore the area with excursions like whale watching (seasonal), diving, or fishing.

Peru is also famous for its food! Explore the diverse restaurants in Mancora, try fresh ceviche (a local specialty), and enjoy the vibrant northern Peruvian cuisine.

For Kids: Children love the beach and the hotel pool. Depending on their age (typically 13+ recommended for lessons), they can also try learning to surf or kitesurf. Many excursions are family-friendly.

What other activities (non-wind) are available?

Beyond surfing, kitesurfing, and yoga, La Maison Eco House and the surrounding area offer many activities:

  • Stand-Up Paddling (SUP)
  • Diving
  • Whale Watching (typically July - October)
  • Cooking Lessons
  • Spa Treatments & Massages
  • Horse Riding on the beach
  • Motocross Enduro

Day Tours can also be arranged: Explore mangroves, visit inland natural thermal tubs, take a ceviche tour, paddle with giant sea turtles, go fishing with local fishermen, or enjoy a yacht trip (seasonal).

Yes, Mancora town has a vibrant nightlife scene with bars and small clubs.

However, La Maison Eco House is located in the quieter Las Pocitas area, offering a choice: you can enjoy a healthy, relaxed retreat vibe, or head into town for more social fun. If you prefer early nights and early mornings, you won't be disturbed by the town's nightlife here. Mancora offers both experiences!

Surfing & Stand Up Paddleboarding

What are the surf conditions like in Mancora?

The Peruvian North Shore is world-famous for its consistent swells and long, tidy waves. Mancora receives both North and South swells throughout the year, offering a wide variety of conditions suitable for different levels.

From the famous long point breaks to gentler waves perfect for learning, there's something for everyone. Key spots nearby include Mancora Point (walking distance!), Lobitos, Cabo Blanco, El Ñuro, and many more.

Water temperature is warm year-round (approx. 20-25°C / 68-77°F), usually requiring only boardshorts or a light top.

Is La Maison Eco House suitable for all levels of surfers?

Yes! We welcome everyone, whether you're a complete beginner wanting to catch your first wave or an experienced shredder looking for perfect point breaks. Our location provides easy access to Mancora's main break, which offers different sections suitable for various levels depending on the swell.

We offer lessons for beginners, coaching (including video analysis) for intermediates and advanced surfers, and guided trips to explore the diverse breaks along the coast.

What surf services are offered?

Through our expert partners based right here, we offer a complete surf experience:

  • Lessons: For beginners learning the basics and intermediates improving technique.
  • Coaching: Advanced coaching for experienced surfers, often including video analysis.
  • Guided Surf Trips: Daily excursions to explore other famous breaks on the North Shore like Lobitos, Cabo Blanco, etc., with local guides.
  • Surf Camps: Immersive packages combining accommodation at La Maison Eco House, meals, lessons/coaching, and sometimes yoga.
  • Board Rentals: A wide selection of boards (soft-tops, longboards, funboards, shortboards) available on-site.

You can add lessons, rentals, or inquire about trips and camps when booking your stay or upon arrival.

What about Stand Up Paddleboarding (SUP)?

Stand Up Paddleboarding (SUP) is a fantastic, accessible water activity, great for fitness, exploring, or just relaxing on the water. It combines balance, strength, and endurance.

We offer various SUP experiences:

  • Initiation/Lessons: Learn the basics of paddling and balance.
  • Mancora Bay Cruises: Paddle along the coast, see the fisherman's pier, and explore towards Las Pocitas.
  • SUP Yoga: Take your yoga practice onto the water for a unique challenge.
  • Tours with Turtles: Guided morning paddles to areas where you can often see giant sea turtles.
  • SUP Surfing: For those looking to catch waves on a paddleboard.

Rentals: Both cruising and surfing SUPs are available for rent by the session or the day.

Kitesurfing

When is the best time/wind season for kitesurfing in Northern Peru?

The main wind season here in Northern Peru (Mancora, Lobitos area) is generally from mid-April to mid-January.

Peru offers year-round wind options, with the South (Paracas) having its best season from November to April.

Our season in the far north (April – December) offers excellent wind statistics. Wind reliability might decrease slightly towards the end of December/early January due to summer conditions.

Wind Consistency Chart (Approx.)
(Strongest & most consistent winds typically Jul - Nov)
What are the wind statistics like on the North Shore?

The region offers fantastic wind reliability during the season (April-December). If participating in day trips to various spots along the coast, the chance of riding is extremely high (often cited above 98%).

The wind typically comes from the southwest, starting around midday (12 PM) and lasting until late afternoon (around 5 PM).

Directly in Mancora town, the statistics might be slightly lower (perhaps 4-5 days a week on average), but it remains a great spot, especially for foiling, potentially allowing you to kitesurf almost every day.

When is the best season for kitesurfing waves?

Since the wind is reliable for much of the year (April-December), the main variable for wave kiting becomes the swell.

During Peru's Austral Winter (roughly May-November), we receive consistent South and Southwest ground swells originating from the South Pacific, often with long periods (up to 22 seconds), which create quality waves.

Ground Swell vs. Wind Swell: Ground swells travel longer distances, resulting in more organized, powerful, and tidy waves compared to local wind swells.

Swell Statistics: Generally, May-July and October-November often see the best swell conditions. Wind strength typically increases from August onwards.

However, great wave sessions can occur throughout the season. April-June is also a favorite for many, as the water is still warm, and it marks the beginning of the season. The Peruvian North Shore offers a great combination of wind and waves, and periods with absolutely no waves are rare and usually short-lived.

Advice for Wave Riders:

1. Come anytime during the main wind season (April-December, perhaps avoiding late Dec/early Jan if waves are the sole focus).

2. A longer stay (10-14 days) increases your chances of catching good swells.

3. If flexible, monitor swell forecasts 1-2 weeks out to potentially time your trip.

What size kites should I bring?

Standard Sizes: For the Peruvian North Shore, a common standard size is a 9m for a ~75kg (165lb) rider and a 7m for a ~60kg (130lb) rider.

Ideal Quiver: Bringing 2 kites is better, and 3 is ideal to cover the range.

Recommendations for a 2-Kite Quiver:

  • May to August: Consider bringing a size larger than your standard kite (e.g., 11m or 12m if your standard is 9m, but generally not bigger than 13m unless you are a heavier rider).
  • September to October: Consider bringing a size smaller than your standard kite (e.g., 7m if your standard is 9m).
  • November to December: Conditions can be variable; sizes like 7m, 9m, and 12m are often used.

Don't want to travel heavy? La Maison Eco House offers premium gear rentals on-site!

What wetsuit do I need?

Water temperature varies slightly through the year (see temperature question). During the Austral winter (approx. May – November), the water in Mancora is typically warm enough (around 20-23°C / 68-73°F) that many riders are comfortable without a wetsuit or just use a light top.

However, if participating in kite trips to southern spots impacted by the Humboldt current, the water can be cooler (down to 18°C / 64°F), and a 3/2mm full suit is recommended.

During the warmer months (approx. December - April), water temperatures are higher, and a wetsuit is generally not needed.

What else should I bring (booties, leash, helmet, etc.)?

Reef Booties: Most surfers and kitesurfers do not wear booties here, especially when launching directly from our sandy beach area in Las Pocitas. However, if you plan to explore other spots or prefer protection, feel free to bring them.

Surf/Kiteboard Leash: Recommended if you plan on wave riding, especially at more advanced spots.

Helmet: While not mandatory or commonly used by all, a helmet is always a good safety measure, especially when learning or pushing your limits. Most incidents involve contact with the board.

Is Mancora and the area good for foiling?

Yes, absolutely. The water deepens fairly quickly just off the shore, providing ample depth for foiling throughout the bay. You can launch from near La Maison Eco House and explore upwind towards the main point break or glide near the pier.

Is Mancora a good place for kiteboarding beginners?

Yes, Mancora is generally a good place for beginners, and lessons are offered right here.

Pros:

  • The typical side-onshore wind direction is considered the safest for learning.
  • The wind is generally very consistent during the season.
  • The beaches are wide and sandy, with fewer crowds than many international spots.

Potential Drawback: When large swells hit the coast (which can happen occasionally, maybe a few days a month during swell season), there isn't always a perfectly sheltered flat-water spot nearby. Lessons might need to be rescheduled if waves become too large (e.g., above shoulder high).

Is wave kitesurfing safe in Peru?

Yes, the Peruvian North Shore wave spots are considered very safe compared to many other destinations, thanks to:

  • Mainly sandy bottoms and beaches.
  • Absence of sharks or other major hazards.
  • Predominantly side-onshore or side-shore winds at most spots (few offshore spots).
  • Steady, clean wind conditions.
  • Typically medium-sized waves (rarely exceeding 2 meters / 6-8 feet, except on exceptional swell days).
  • Experienced local guides and schools prioritizing safety (offering support, rescue, first aid).
What are the requirements for joining guided kite trips/safaris?

Guided wave trips cater to both experienced wave riders and those looking to get started in waves.

However, participants need solid general kite control. You should be comfortable and able to:

  • Ride consistently upwind.
  • Perform basic jumps.
  • Ride toeside.
  • Body drag upwind to recover your board.
  • Know self-rescue procedures (a refresher can be provided).
Can freeriders or freestylers join the kite trips?

While the focus of many trips is wave riding due to the quality of the spots, freeriders and freestylers are absolutely welcome! There's plenty of space outside the main wave breaks to practice jumps or simply cruise.

Travel Information

How do I get to Mancora?

It’s easy! There are 2 main ways to get to Máncora and the north of Peru from abroad:

a) Via Lima (LIM), the Peruvian capital city, or

b) Via Guayaquil (GYE), South Ecuador.

Most guests find traveling through Lima easier, as the only option from Guayaquil to Mancora involves a night bus crossing the border into Peru late at night.

Consider your options when choosing Lima or Guayaquil. Flights to Guayaquil might sometimes be cheaper, especially if you are traveling from the north of Latin America.

From Southern Europe: Check flights from Madrid (often low-cost options available, but check baggage policies for large surf quivers). Flights typically go to Lima.

From Northern Europe: Direct flights to Lima are available from cities like Paris and Amsterdam. These are often less expensive than flying to Guayaquil.

From the USA: While flying to Guayaquil and taking a bus is possible, it involves a long bus journey and is often chosen for budget reasons. Flying to Lima is generally easier. Direct (nonstop) flights to Lima (LIM) are available from major hubs like Atlanta, Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, Los Angeles, Miami, and New York.

Once in Lima, should I fly or take a bus to Mancora?

From Lima, you can reach the Mancora area by domestic flight or by bus.

Bus: The bus journey takes around 16 hours. While long, you travel mostly overnight on the straight Pan-American highway. Premium bus companies offer comfortable services like 180° reclining seats, Wi-Fi, personal screens, and meals.

Flight: Flying is significantly faster (approx. 1.5-hour flight plus airport transfer time).

The best choice depends on your flight connections from home, your available time, and your budget.

Which airports are closest to Mancora?

There are several airports serving the Mancora region:

  • Punta Sal (Mancora Aerodrome - Walter Braedt Segú): A small aerodrome just 5 minutes outside Mancora, currently served by ATSA Airlines. Check their flight schedules directly.
  • Talara (TYL - Cap. FAP Víctor Montes Arias Int'l): The closest major airport, about 1 hour 10 minutes away by taxi.
  • Tumbes (TBP - Cap. FAP Pedro Canga Rodríguez Int'l): About 1 hour 45 minutes away by taxi.
  • Piura (PIU - Cap. FAP Guillermo Concha Iberico Int'l): About 2 hours 30 minutes away by taxi.

Which airlines offer domestic flights in Peru?

The main domestic airlines in Peru include LATAM, Sky Airline Peru, and JetSMART Peru. Viva Air ceased operations. For flights directly to the small Mancora aerodrome, check ATSA Airlines.

Recommendations:

a) LATAM often offers reliable service. It's generally best to buy tickets directly from the airline's official website.

b) When booking flights from home, try to book through to your final domestic destination (Talara, Tumbes, or Piura). However, ensure you have sufficient layover time in Lima (at least 2-3 hours is recommended), as you will need to collect your luggage, pass through immigration/customs (if arriving internationally), and re-check in for your domestic flight.

How do I get to La Maison Eco House from the airport?

The easiest way is by taxi. La Maison Eco House can organize a trusted taxi transfer for you upon request. Alternatively, you can find official taxis inside the airport terminals (refer to the approximate prices in the airport question above).

Taxi Sharing Tip: You might be able to share a taxi by finding other passengers heading to Mancora or asking drivers outside the terminal ("Quiero compartir taxi"), potentially reducing the cost.

Other Options (Less Recommended):

  • "Colectivo" vans are available but generally not recommended for comfort or safety.
  • Local buses (like EPPO) are safer but slower. From Talara, you could take a moto-taxi to the bus terminal for frequent buses to Mancora. From Piura or Tumbes, the bus journey is significantly longer, making a taxi the preferred option.
Which bus company should I use from Lima to Mancora?

We recommend premium bus companies like Cruz del Sur, Oltursa, or ExcluCiva for the long journey from Lima.

Tips for Bus Travel:

  • Choose the second floor for potentially better views and specify 180° reclining seats ("cama") if available.
  • Seats in the front half of the bus often experience less shaking.
  • Consider the left side going north (towards Mancora) and the right side returning south for potential sea views.

Avoid the cheapest bus alternatives for safety and comfort reasons.

Buses typically leave Lima between 4 PM and 6 PM, arriving in Mancora the following morning (around 9 AM - 10 AM).

Fares are approximately 160 soles (~50 USD) one-way. Booking online a few days in advance is recommended.

You can easily book your return ticket once you are in Mancora.

What should I check regarding money before traveling?

It's wise to prepare your finances before your trip:

  • Inform your bank about your travel dates to avoid blocked cards.
  • Check the daily/weekly withdrawal limits on your ATM/credit cards and consider requesting a temporary increase if needed.
  • Inquire about foreign transaction fees and ATM withdrawal fees charged by your bank. Some cards offer lower or no fees.
  • Consider bringing two different cards (stored separately) as a backup.
  • Bringing some cash (USD is widely accepted for larger payments and easily exchanged) can be useful to avoid excessive ATM fees, especially as Peru is relatively safe for travelers taking standard precautions.
Are there ATMs in Mancora?

Yes, there are several ATMs available in Mancora town.

Be aware that Peruvian ATMs often have a withdrawal limit per transaction (e.g., around $200-$400 USD equivalent, though this can vary). Additionally, both the local ATM and your home bank may charge fees for each withdrawal. Relying solely on ATM withdrawals can become costly and might be limited by daily/weekly card limits.

What currency is used? Should I bring USD or Soles?

The official currency is the Peruvian Nuevo Sol (PEN or S/.).

Soles are used for most daily expenses (small shops, local meals, moto-taxis).

US Dollars (USD) are often accepted for larger expenses like hotels, tours, and nicer restaurants. Prices for tourism services are sometimes quoted directly in USD.

Recommendation: Bring a mix. Have enough Soles for daily incidentals. Bring USD (newer, undamaged bills preferred) for larger payments and easy exchange. Exchanging USD to Soles is straightforward in Mancora, though rates might be slightly less favorable than in Lima.

How can I pay at La Maison Eco House?

Our prices are typically quoted in US Dollars (USD) for clarity. You can usually settle your final bill using USD or Peruvian Soles (PEN). We use fair, current exchange rates if converting between currencies. Please confirm accepted payment methods (cash, card, transfer) when booking.

How and where should I exchange currency?

Exchanging currency at Lima's airport upon arrival is convenient but often offers poor rates. Airport taxis usually accept USD.

If spending time in Lima, exchange rates are generally better there. Use official exchange houses ("Casas de Cambio") rather than street money changers for better security and rates.

In Mancora, you can exchange USD at banks or authorized exchange points. La Maison Eco House staff can advise on reliable options.

What if I have Euros or other currencies?

Euros (EUR) might be exchangeable in major cities like Lima or tourist hubs like Mancora, but rates may not be optimal, and acceptance is less common than USD.

If arriving directly to Mancora without passing through Lima, it's generally best to bring US Dollars obtained in your home country.
